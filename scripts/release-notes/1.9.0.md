## What's new on the Mac

**A tour of Murmur.** The first time you open Murmur after this update (or right after setup), a starling shows you around the real app. It points at each part, explains what it does, and lets you try dictation, a rewrite and file search on the spot. For meeting notes it shows the real offer card and notes panel: Live answers, Notes, Transcript and Context. It ends with where the AI runs, on your Mac or in the cloud, and what a cloud model sees. It moves on only when you click, and it starts by itself just once. Take it again any time from About & Help or **Help › Take the Tour**.

**Tap to write.** Click in any text box and tap the talk key once. Murmur writes what goes there: your reply to the conversation above it, the message a note in the box asks for ("say yes but after 5, ask if he needs the enterprise tier"), or your draft, finished and fixed. It reads that window and asks your commands' AI. It brings in what it knows about you only when the conversation is about it, and leaves a gap like [time] rather than making up a fact. Nothing is ever sent: it's in the box, and ⌘Z takes it back. A tap outside a text box, in an address bar or in a search field does nothing. Turn it off under **Settings › Shortcuts › Tap to write**.

**Your memory, as pages.** With screen memory on, Murmur now keeps a page for each person and project you work with, a digest of what's going on (projects, people, promises), and a short profile of how you work. Each claim on a page says how it's known and where it came from. The pages are rewritten as new evidence comes in, and old claims are kept as history.

**Your memory in your AI apps.** Claude Desktop, Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, VS Code, Windsurf, Gemini CLI, LM Studio and Cline can ask Murmur what you know: "what did I promise Priya?". Murmur adds itself to the ones on your Mac (their settings are backed up first), and nothing leaves your Mac except the answers they ask for. Turn it off with **Share memory with AI apps** in Settings.

## Also on the Mac

- Duplicate projects ("Sudoku V2" and "Modernizing Sudoku v2 game") are merged into one, with their memories and pages.
- With "not at weekends" on, Murmur no longer reads the screen on Saturdays when your calendar counts only Sunday as the weekend.
- Cloud models get room for a whole page, and an answer that's cut off keeps its finished part.

## Windows

No changes in this release. The tour, tap to write and memory pages come to Windows next.
